Step 1: 5-7 Years Without a Diagnosis

For a child rare genetic disorder detection can take five to seven years [2]. The signs are buried in tables containing miles of genetic data, and physical signs are not obvious [2].

Doctor's Perspective The reality of testing is that 'sick' is often not sick enough. When diagnostic panels pan out empty, families go through agonizing limbo, causing severe stress and clinical burnout.
Engineer's Perspective The biggest technical challenge is the vast scale of information—thousands of variants per patient are referenced against free-text clinical notes [2]. Secondary analyses using AI reasoning assistants and multi-step language-model workflows applied to unsolved genomes find undiscovered signals [2].

Step 2: Real-World Clinical Deployment

Rather than abstract models, generative AI has been deployed in high-stakes environments—such as Boston Children's Hospital, where AI tools successfully helped diagnose 18 children whose rare conditions previously stumped specialists [5].

Doctor's Perspective Clinicians must review phenotypes and genotypes side-by-side, using AI models as structured second-opinion engines rather than autonomous decision-makers [2].
Engineer's Perspective Agentic systems ingest symptom codes and complex variant structures as input data, generating transparent reasoning traces for clinical auditing [2, 5].

Step 3: The Risks of AI Fabrication

Web text used to train commercial language models is rife with unvetted data and false information [7]. Investigative auditing by Stokel-Walker revealed that AI models were misled by a prompt regarding a self-created fake illness called "Bixonimania," citing manufactured references as real medical facts [7]. Unmoderated systems ingested this misinformation and treated it as legitimate healthcare guidance [7].

Try toggling the RAG check and Human review buttons at the top to see how safety systems protect AI from hallucinations, with the 98% illustrative confidence score demonstrating how convincing an unvetted error can look [7].

Step 4: Performing the Safety Checks

A base model does not roll out safely on its own [3, 4]. Two fundamental protections must be enforced:

  • RAG check. Cross-references model output against trusted medical sources (like PubMed and OMIM) before generating text [9].
  • Clinician-in-the-Loop. Requires an expert human clinician to authenticate every diagnostic hypothesis and treatment plan [2, 3].
Engineer's Perspective Retrieval is performed before generation to intercept low-confidence or unverified responses, routing them to a queue for human review [9].
Doctor's Perspective The AI output serves strictly as a prompt for clinical judgment, never an automatic prescription [2].

Step 5: Evidence and Accuracy

Hao and Wang's study incorporating RAG into clinical workflows noted an increase in documentation accuracy from 93.25% to 99.25%, with preoperative diagnostic accuracy reaching 96.4% [9].

Still, mistakes persist in intricate clinical cases, suggesting that while RAG minimizes risk, it cannot eliminate all inaccuracies [4, 9]. Rigorous human oversight is essential. Over 90% of clinicians state that technology adoption is limited by trust and verification concerns [8, 9].
